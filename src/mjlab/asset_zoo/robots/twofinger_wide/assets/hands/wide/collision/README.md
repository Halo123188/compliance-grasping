# Alternate collision geometry

**Nothing here is wired into the simulation.** The task builds its collision
geometry from fitted axis-aligned boxes (`hand_urdf.fit_collision_boxes`), and
that is unchanged. This directory holds an alternate representation, generated
so the choice can be made on measurements rather than on intuition.

## The three sets

COACD decompositions of the whole gripper at three concavity thresholds,
generated by `tools/coacd_hand.py`. Pick one; they are alternatives, not layers.

| set | base_link | adapter | shim | left_1 | left_2 | right_1 | right_2 | **total** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `coacd_t0.15/` | 3 | 4 | 6 | 9 | 5 | 5 | 6 | **38** |
| `coacd_t0.2/` | 3 | 4 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | **23** |
| `coacd_t0.4/` | 3 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2 | **14** |

Volume inflation at 0.2, per part: base_link +34%, adapter +38%, shim +24%,
left_1 +32%, left_2 +32%, right_1 +36%, right_2 +34%.

Hulls are in each **part's own frame**, matching the STL they came from, so a
URDF or MJCF can reference them directly. `manifest.json` maps part → files.

For comparison, the decomposition shipped in `robot-actuator-model` is **149
hulls** at threshold 0.05 (0.1 for hardware) — so these are 4×, 6.5× and 10.6×
fewer respectively.

## Why the adapter has its own threshold

At the global 0.20 the adapter took **14 hulls** — more than four times any
other part — all of it spent resolving bolt holes in a flat plate that is welded
inside the wrist and that nothing ever touches. 0.30 gives 4 hulls.

It is **not** capped with `max_convex_hull`, and that is the interesting part:

| adapter setting | hulls | volume error |
|---|---:|---:|
| threshold 0.20 | 14 | +21.1% |
| **threshold 0.30** | **4** | **+38.2%** |
| threshold 0.40 | 1 | +45.2% |
| `max_convex_hull=3` @ 0.20 | 3 | **+58.0%** |

Capping at 3 is **worse than not splitting at all** — 3 hulls enclosing +58%
against a single convex hull's +45%. The cap truncates the split partway and
leaves whichever pieces the traversal happened to emit first, which is not a
decomposition chosen to be good at 3. Asking for a *number* gets you a number;
asking for a *tolerance* gets you a decomposition.

This is the same mechanism that left `left_1` at exactly 32 in the shipped set:
that is not "32 was enough", it is "the budget ran out".

## What this does and does not fix

Measured across thresholds from 339 hulls down to 2, on `right_1`, `right_2`
and `base_link`:

- **Volume error** responds to threshold: 14–16% at 0.01–0.05, ~30% at 0.2,
  56–64% at 0.4. That is concavity being bridged.
- **The two surfaces this task actually loads do not move at all.** The pad face
  that contacts the cube and the fingertip that must clear the foam sit at a
  constant **+0.34–0.52 mm** at *every* threshold. There is no density at which
  they improve.

That constant offset is not the decomposition's fault. Exactly three parts are
not watertight — **`base_link`, `left_1`, `right_1`** — so COACD must remesh
them first, and the remesh is where the offset comes from.
`preprocess_mode="off"` segfaults on them, which is the library saying the same
thing.

The defect is **not holes**: all nine meshes have **zero boundary edges**. It is
non-manifold edges (shared by more than two faces) — 162 on `base_link`, 107 on
each proximal link. Roughly half are exact duplicate triangles (80/40/40);
removing them is lossless (`dvol = 0.000000 cm³`) and fixes about half the
edges. Full trimesh repair gets no further, because the rest is genuine
self-touching geometry and no tool can know whether two surfaces meeting at a
seam should become one solid.

Negative `euler_number` is NOT the defect and does not indicate damage: it is
genus, i.e. through-holes in the part. `adapter` is euler −22 (genus 12) and
perfectly watertight — that is just a plate with bolt holes.

The three failing parts split into 183, 92 and 92 connected components, so each
STL is an assembly of sub-solids exported together. Unioning those sub-solids
**within a link** is what would fix it, and it does not touch articulation —
they are all rigidly part of the same link. (`left_2` has 2 components and is
watertight, so multi-body alone is fine; the failure is sub-solids that
intersect.)

**So the fitted boxes are still more accurate than any COACD setting on the
contact surfaces** (0.00 mm, because they are fitted to exact mesh bounds), and
COACD is ~7× better on interior volume. They are accurate about different
things:

- flat pad on a flat cube face → boxes are exact, COACD is 0.35 mm out
- concavity, off-nominal contact, finger-on-finger → COACD, by a wide margin

The highest-value fix is neither: **make the meshes watertight upstream in the
CAD export**. That removes the 0.35 mm floor and improves both representations
at once.

## Regenerating

```bash
python tools/coacd_hand.py --threshold 0.2 \
    --out viz/hand_coacd \
    --export src/cubegrasp_tf/assets/hands/wide/collision/coacd_coarse
```

Per-part overrides live in `THRESHOLD_OVERRIDE` at the top of that script, and
are applied as a **floor** — `max(global, override)`. An override below the
global threshold would make the adapter finer than the parts that actually
grasp, which is backwards; at global 0.40 it silently would have.

```bash
for T in 0.15 0.2 0.4; do
  python tools/coacd_hand.py --threshold $T --out viz/hand_coacd \
      --export src/cubegrasp_tf/assets/hands/wide/collision/coacd_t$T
done
```
